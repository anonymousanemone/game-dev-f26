It identifies the source collection as **University of California**, the scanner as **Google**, and the Google Books ID as `RQE2AQAAMAAJ`. The [Public Domain Mark explanation](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/) allows copying, adaptation and redistribution, including commercial use, without copyright permission. This supplies an affirmative public-domain reuse basis for artwork taken from this copy; it is a rights-status mark rather than a CC license grant.

Credit for this copy: **Carl Richard Lepsius (ed.), *Das Todtenbuch der Ägypter nach dem hieroglyphischen Papyrus in Turin*. Leipzig: G. Wigand, 1842. University of California copy, digitized by Google; Internet Archive, `bub_gb_RQE2AQAAMAAJ`; Public Domain Mark 1.0.**

### Citation for the underlying papyrus

The Totenbuch archive identifies P. Turin 1791 as **TM 57201**, Museo Egizio, Turin, and lists spells 31 and 32 with vignettes. Its [object page](https://totenbuch.awk.nrw.de/objekt/tm57201) supplies this citation: **Totenbuchprojekt Bonn, TM 57201, https://totenbuch.awk.nrw.de/objekt/tm57201** (accessed October 26, 2026). Use this for the object description; retain the Lepsius DOI above for the printed/digitized edition used as the visual reference. The object page separately flags restricted image material.

The archive's [documentation](https://totenbuch.awk.nrw.de/projekt/dokumentation#OAIPMH) recommends stable `/objekt/tm…` addresses for citations. OAI-PMH supplies object metadata; unAPI supplies bibliographic exports, including BibTeX. Those interfaces document retrieval and citation, not permission to republish images. The documentation's persistent-citation section is the relevant guidance for this object.

### Reuse terms actually linked by the Heidelberg item

The record's `dc:rights` values are `info:eu-repo/semantics/openAccess` and a link to Heidelberg's standard copyright terms. Its download links specifically to [Propylaeum-DOK — Terms of Use / No POD](https://archiv.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/propylaeumdok/help/license_urhg.html); the machine-readable metadata points to the [equivalent heiDOK terms](https://archiv.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/volltextserver/help/license_urhg.html). Both were retrieved and checked.

These are **not a Creative Commons license or a Public Domain Mark for this item**. The linked notice limits reproductions to private/personal use under German copyright law, prohibits alteration, and says further public reproduction/distribution needs the rights holder's approval. That is the notice attached to the repository item; it does not by itself establish which rights, if any, still subsist in the 1842 artwork or its reproduction. The historical work and the modern repository's notice must be distinguished. Open access alone is not evidence of permission to redistribute cropped images in a public game repository. This research records the provenance and terms; it does not certify publication clearance. Resolve that remaining rights question with the instructor or documented applicable permission/basis before treating it as cleared.
